## 1. Can Transplanted Hair Follicles Survive in the Absence of Arrector Pili Muscle Attachments?

*Dermatologic surgery : official publication for American Society for Dermatologic Surgery [et al.]* · 2026-08-31 · `植髮手術`
> FitzGerald H, Morriss S, Nirenberg A, Bhoyrul B

BACKGROUND: The arrector pili muscle (APM) is a key structural component of the pilosebaceous unit and follicular stem cell niche. However, its fate after hair transplantation (HT) remains poorly understood.

OBJECTIVE: To histologically assess APM preservation in transplanted hair follicles (HFs).

METHODS: Scalp biopsies from recipient and native donor areas of 5 patients with suboptimal graft survival after HT were analyzed. Two 4-mm punch biopsies were obtained in each case for horizontal and vertical sectioning. The presence of APMs associated with follicular units (FUs), proximal and distal APM attachment sites, and peri-APM inflammation were evaluated.
