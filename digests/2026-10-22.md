BACKGROUND: Mast cells are key regulators of cutaneous inflammation and are known to accumulate in response to various immunological stimuli. Although their role in several dermatological conditions has been established, their specific contribution to different forms of alopecia-particularly scarring versus nonscarring types-remains incompletely understood. This study aimed to compare the degree of mast cell infiltration across three distinct types of alopecia: androgenetic alopecia (AGA), fibrosing alopecia in a pattern distribution (FAPD), and lichen planopilaris (LPP).

METHOD: A total of 56 patients diagnosed with AGA, FAPD, or LPP were enrolled. Scalp biopsies were obtained from all participants, and diagnoses were confirmed through histopathological examination.
